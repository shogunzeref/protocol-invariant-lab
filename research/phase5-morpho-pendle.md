# Phase 5 -- Pendle PT collateral in Morpho Blue markets

Investigating whether Pendle Principal Tokens used as Morpho Blue collateral
create a structural valuation mismatch that becomes economically exploitable
under price distortion and recursive leverage.

Every claim below is labelled:

- **FACT** -- read directly from source or from a read-only chain call, and cited.
- **INFERENCE** -- follows from several facts plus an interpretation the facts
  alone do not force.
- **HYPOTHESIS** -- proposed and tested here; the outcome is stated.
- **UNKNOWN** -- not established by this work.

Provenance for all source claims is in
[`phase5-morpho-pendle-sources.md`](phase5-morpho-pendle-sources.md). The
per-market reconnaissance is in
[`morpho-pendle-market-map.md`](morpho-pendle-market-map.md). All numbers quoted
here are regenerated by `node scripts/phase5/run-experiments.js` into
`research/data/phase5-results.json`.

**Scope and safety.** No transaction was sent to any live protocol. No position
was opened, no asset borrowed, no oracle touched, no liquidation triggered. Live
interaction was limited to `eth_call`, `eth_blockNumber` and a public GraphQL
indexer. The manipulation analysis is closed-form and numerical, in
`models/`; there is no deployable attack contract in this repository, and the
models are plain JavaScript with no on-chain counterpart.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The hypothesis this phase set out to test is **not supported**. The mismatch it
predicted exists and is measurable, but it is roughly three orders of magnitude
too small to exploit, and the reason is structural rather than incidental.

What was established:

1. **FACT** -- Morpho Blue transmits oracle price into borrowing power with
   elasticity exactly 1. There is no cap, band, damping or sanity check between
   `IOracle.price()` and the health check. Confirmed against
   `morpho-blue/src/Morpho.sol` and reproduced in `models/morpho-market.js`.
   Hypothesis A's *mechanism* is real.

2. **FACT** -- but no live Morpho PT market reads an untimed AMM spot price. Of
   66 markets found, 28 price PT through a 900s/1800s implied-rate TWAP, 17
   through a market-independent linear discount, 12 through the `min()` of both,
   and 9 through external feeds that could not be verified. The count of untimed
   spot reads across all 66 classified oracle paths is **zero**. In the lab's
   earlier Moola phases that count was 1, and that is precisely what made the
   earlier attack work.

3. **FACT** -- the Pendle AMM cannot price a PT above par at any cost.
   `MarketMathCore._getExchangeRate` reverts with `MarketExchangeRateBelowOne`
   whenever the asset-per-PT rate would drop below 1. So the maximum upward
   displacement available on a Pendle market is exactly `1/P - 1`, and it
   collapses toward zero as the PT converges to par.

4. **INFERENCE, and the central result** -- combining (1) and (3) gives a closed
   form for when displacement can cause insolvency at all. A maximally-borrowed
   position owes `C * P_oracle * LLTV`; independently it is worth `C * P_true`.
   Insolvency therefore requires displacement `d > 1/LLTV - 1`, while the
   ceiling supplies at most `1/P_true - 1`. The attack needs

   ```
   1/P_true - 1  >  1/LLTV - 1     <=>     P_true < LLTV
   ```

   So displacement-driven insolvency is unreachable while a PT trades above its
   market's LLTV -- independent of attacker capital, pool liquidity and loop
   depth. **All 23 live unexpired markets with a verifiable feed sit on the safe
   side.** For the anchor market the required displacement is 9.29% against a
   ceiling of 3.44%.

   **This boundary is not universal, and saying otherwise would overstate the
   result.** `P_PT < LLTV` needs no credit event -- only a long maturity and a
   high implied rate. Re-priced to a 365-day horizon at the anchor's own 10.25%
   implied rate, PT is worth 0.9026, which is *below* both the 91.5% and 94.5%
   LLTVs in live use. 120 of the 720 stress rows sit in that regime.

5. **FACT** -- in that regime a *second, independent* defence binds instead: the
   Pendle curve's 96% PT proportion cap. Long-dated PT has plenty of room to par
   but the curve still refuses to get there.

   | Horizon | PT price | Required (LLTV 94.5%) | Available to par | Max achievable on curve |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | 365d | 0.9026 | 5.82% | 10.79% | **2.0%** |
   | 180d | 0.9507 | 5.82% | 5.18% | 1.0% |
   | 90d | 0.9751 | 5.82% | 2.56% | 0.5% |
   | 30d | 0.9916 | 5.82% | 0.85% | none |

   **INFERENCE.** Which defence protects a market depends on its maturity: near
   expiry the par ceiling binds, and long-dated the curve's proportion cap binds.
   The two overlap across the whole observed range, but they are different
   mechanisms with different failure modes -- the par ceiling is arithmetic and
   cannot be bought off, whereas the proportion cap is a function of pool depth
   and composition. A long-dated, high-implied-rate PT on a high-LLTV market has
   **no par-ceiling margin at all**, and rests on Pendle pool depth alone.

6. **FACT** -- the observed defences are quantitatively far stronger than the
   boundary above even requires. On the anchor market a 0.5% price move costs
   ~$7.8k and needs 12.6% of the pool's PT; held for one block against a 900s
   TWAP it transmits 1/75 of itself into the oracle, so the *reachable* oracle
   displacement from a single-block operation is under 1bp.

7. **Genuine positive finding** -- there *is* a real valuation gap, just not an
   exploitable one: the oracle marks PT at the curve price while a liquidator
   unwinding the position realises less, because a PT's only native venue is its
   own Pendle pool. At the anchor market a position equal to 1% of the pool
   carries a 26bp unwind discount, rising with position size. This sits inside
   the liquidation incentive at every size tested up to 20% of the pool, so it is
   a **parameter-sizing consideration, not a vulnerability**.

8. Across a 720-row stress surface sweeping oracle distortion x maturity x
   liquidity x LLTV x loop depth: **0 rows profitable for the attacker, 0 rows
   insolvent against an independent mark, 0 rows with a protocol shortfall**, and
   504 of the 576 displacement targets not achievable on the curve at any price.
   The best outcome available to an attacker anywhere on the surface is a loss of
   $132.

**This is not a new vulnerability class.** It is closest to the stale/lagging
oracle class, with the sign reversed: the linear-discount feeds lag *downward*,
which is conservative for the protocol.

## 3. Actual architecture

**FACT.** The chain from PT to borrowing power, as classified by probing each
deployed contract's selectors (not by contract names or indexer labels):

```
PT-reUSD-10DEC2026 (collateral token, Morpho market 0x1e9d6146...)
  |
  v  Morpho.borrow -> _isHealthy -> IOracle.price()
MorphoChainlinkOracleV2  0x217d6DdCDB95112C51657F6270e8C079CFDB51f0
  |    scaleFactor 1e18, BASE_FEED_1 ->
  v
OjoPTFeed                       (aggregates its legs with min())
  |
  +--> PendleChainlinkOracle          -> Pendle market 0x13285bCb..., 900s TWAP of ln(implied rate)
  |
  +--> PendleLinearDiscountOracleWrapper
         -> PendleSparkLinearDiscountOracle   -> 1 - baseDiscountPerYear * T, no market input
```

Three architectures account for all 57 verifiable markets:

| Architecture | Markets | PT price source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PendleChainlinkOracle` only | 28 | 900s or 1800s TWAP of the Pendle market's ln(implied rate) |
| `PendleSparkLinearDiscountOracle` only | 17 | `1 - discount * T`; reads no market state at all |
| `min()` of both, via `OjoPTFeed` | 12 | whichever is lower |
| External / unverified | 9 | UNKNOWN -- see section 18 |

**FACT.** Several markets wrap the above in a `MetaOracleDeviationTimelock`
holding a primary and a backup `MorphoChainlinkOracleV2`, gated by a deviation
threshold and challenge/healing timelocks. **INFERENCE:** this is a governance
circuit breaker on *which feed is authoritative*, not a filter on an individual
feed's value; it bounds how quickly a compromised feed can take effect but does
not smooth the price a healthy feed reports.

## 4. Oracle implementation

For each mechanism, the inputs, units, and dependence structure.

### 4.1 `MorphoChainlinkOracleV2` (the adapter Morpho calls)

**FACT.**

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Returns | collateral quoted in loan token, scaled `1e36`, decimals differential absorbed |
| Inputs | up to four Chainlink-compatible feeds plus two optional ERC-4626 vaults |
| Time dependence | none of its own; inherits its feeds' |
| Maturity dependence | none of its own |
| Liquidity dependence | none of its own |
| Deviation limits | **none** |
| Fallback behaviour | **none** -- it reverts if a leg reverts |
| Manipulation resistance | none of its own; it is pure composition |

The `1e36` scaling and the decimals differential are pinned by test in
`test/phase5/morpho-propagation.js`; a 6-decimal PT against an 18-decimal loan
token is a 1e12 error if mishandled.

### 4.2 `PendleChainlinkOracle` (TWAP leg)

**FACT.**

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Inputs | Pendle market observation buffer (cumulative ln implied rate), `twapDuration` |
| Computation | `lnRate = (cum[now] - cum[now - w]) / w`, then `P = exp(-lnRate * T)` |
| Units | 18-decimal asset-per-PT |
| Observation window | 900s on every market observed but one, which uses 1800s |
| Observation cardinality | 83 to 1088 across the observed markets |
| Time dependence | strong -- averages over the window |
| Maturity dependence | strong -- `T` enters the exponent directly |
| Liquidity dependence | indirect -- the implied rate is a curve output, so displacing it costs liquidity-proportional capital |
| Deviation limits | none |
| Staleness guard | 86400s on the anchor's `OjoPTFeed` |
| Manipulation resistance | dilution by `heldSeconds / twapDuration` |

The port of this path reproduces the live on-chain oracle answer for **23 of 23**
comparable markets, to a maximum relative error of **3.6e-5** and a median of
**3.5e-6**. The residual is expected and is not numerical error: the on-chain
value is a TWAP, the modelled value is the spot rate implied by the last trade.

### 4.3 `PendleSparkLinearDiscountOracle` (discount leg)

**FACT.**

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Inputs | `baseDiscountPerYear`, `maturity`, block timestamp |
| Computation | `P = 1 - baseDiscountPerYear * T`, floored at maturity to exactly 1 |
| Observed `baseDiscountPerYear` | 0.20/yr and 0.30/yr |
| Time dependence | linear in `T` only |
| Liquidity dependence | **none** |
| Manipulation resistance | **total** -- it reads no market state, so its manipulation cost is infinite |

**INFERENCE.** This feed is not merely manipulation-resistant, it is
systematically *conservative*: a straight line strictly below the exponential the
market prices. At a 90-day horizon on the anchor market it marks PT at 0.9507
(0.20/yr) or 0.9260 (0.30/yr) against a market price of 0.9751 -- a deliberate
2.4 to 4.9 point haircut that costs borrowers capacity and buys the protocol
margin.

### 4.4 `OjoPTFeed` (aggregation)

**FACT.** Aggregates its legs with `min()`. **INFERENCE:** this is the binding
defence on the 12 markets that use it, and it is stronger than the TWAP. An
attacker buying PT pushes the TWAP leg *up*; `min()` then simply returns the
discount leg, which cannot be moved at all. Raising the reported price requires
raising the *lower* leg, and the lower leg is usually the one with no market
input.

## 5. PT valuation mechanics

**FACT.** Four values that must not be conflated, all implemented separately in
`models/pendle-pt-valuation.js`:

| Value | Definition | Anchor market, live state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deterministic redemption | 1 asset per PT at or after expiry; a hard ceiling before it | 1.000000 |
| Market-implied | `exp(-lnImpliedRate * T)` from the curve's stored rate | 0.966743 |
| Oracle-reported | what Morpho actually reads | 0.966747 (TWAP leg) |
| Independently realizable | proceeds of actually selling the position through the pool | 0.964190 at 1% of pool |

The ordering `redemption >= market-implied > realizable` holds at every maturity
tested, and is asserted rather than assumed in
`test/phase5/pt-valuation-maturity.js`. The oracle value is *not* assumed equal
to any of the others; the entire question is where it sits.

## 6. Maturity analysis

`V_PT = f(T, P_market, R, L)`, swept over the required horizons on the anchor
market with the implied rate held fixed. Maturity is varied by moving `expiry`,
not `blockTime`, so reserve composition is preserved and `T` is isolated.

| T | Market-implied | TWAP oracle | Realizable (1% of pool) | Oracle - realizable | Linear 0.20/yr | Linear 0.30/yr |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 365d | 0.902616 | 0.902616 | 0.895454 | +0.007161 | 0.800000 | 0.700000 |
| 180d | 0.950728 | 0.950728 | 0.946991 | +0.003737 | 0.901370 | 0.852055 |
| 90d | 0.975053 | 0.975053 | 0.973132 | +0.001921 | 0.950685 | 0.926027 |
| 30d | 0.991614 | 0.991614 | 0.990962 | +0.000652 | 0.983562 | 0.975342 |
| 14d | 0.996078 | 0.996078 | 0.995772 | +0.000306 | 0.992329 | 0.988493 |
| 7d | 0.998037 | 0.998037 | 0.997884 | +0.000153 | 0.996164 | 0.994247 |
| 1d | 0.999719 | 0.999719 | 0.999697 | +0.000022 | 0.999452 | 0.999178 |

**FACT.** The oracle-vs-realizable gap **narrows monotonically toward maturity**,
by a factor of ~325 from 365d to 1d. This answers the brief's question directly
and in the opposite direction to the hypothesis: risk from this mismatch is
concentrated in *long-dated* PT, not near expiry.

**FACT.** The available upward displacement collapses in the same direction,
because the par ceiling closes in:

| T | Market price | Max upward displacement (`1/P - 1`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 365d | 0.902616 | 10.79% |
| 180d | 0.950728 | 5.18% |
| 90d | 0.975053 | 2.56% |
| 30d | 0.991614 | 0.85% |
| 14d | 0.996078 | 0.39% |
| 7d | 0.998037 | 0.20% |
| 1d | 0.999719 | **0.03%** |

**INFERENCE.** Near maturity a PT is not merely expensive to manipulate upward,
it is *arithmetically impossible*: at 7 days even the brief's smallest 0.5% target
exceeds the entire headroom to par. The near-maturity regime the hypothesis
identified as most dangerous is the safest one.

**FACT.** Several scanned markets reference PTs already past expiry, and their
oracles report exactly par, as expected.

## 7. AMM liquidity model

**FACT.** `models/pendle-amm.js` is a direct port of Pendle's
`MarketMathCore`: `getMarketPreCompute` (rate scalar, rate anchor re-anchoring
from the stored implied rate), `_getExchangeRate` (log-proportion curve, the 96%
proportion cap, the below-par revert), `calcTrade` (asymmetric pre/post-fee
handling for buys versus sells), and `_setNewMarketStateTrade`.

Validation, in `test/phase5/pendle-amm-port.js`:

- Reproduces the live on-chain oracle answer on 23/23 markets to <=3.6e-5.
- Buying PT raises the price, selling lowers it.
- Round trips always lose money -- there is no fee-free path through the curve.
- The PT price never exceeds par at any trade size up to 95% of the pool.
- Matured PTs price at par.

**Caveat, stated plainly.** The port uses IEEE doubles where Pendle uses
`int256` fixed-point with directional rounding. Agreement to 3.6e-5 against live
values bounds the error empirically at four orders of magnitude below the
smallest effect discussed here, but the port is not a bit-exact reimplementation
and should not be used as one.

## 8. Oracle distortion model

### 8.1 Market displacement cost `C(dP, T, L)`

Cost of moving the anchor market's PT price, solved by bisection on the curve.
`Capital` is what must be deployed; `Cost` is what is unrecoverable on the round
trip (fees plus slippage). Full 147-row surface, including the 0.25x and 4x
liquidity scalings, in `research/data/phase5-results.json`.

| T | Target | Binding constraint | PT needed (share of pool) | Capital | Round-trip cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 365d | 0.5% | -- | 12.55% | $383,907 | $7,764 |
| 365d | 1.0% | -- | 24.01% | $738,055 | $18,672 |
| 365d | 2.0% | -- | 43.52% | $1,350,932 | $47,467 |
| 365d | 3.0% | proportion cap | -- | -- | unreachable |
| 365d | >=20% | **par ceiling** | -- | -- | unreachable |
| 180d | 0.5% | -- | 23.03% | $738,724 | $9,241 |
| 180d | 1.0% | -- | 42.20% | $1,360,719 | $23,772 |
| 180d | >=2.0% | proportion cap / par ceiling | -- | -- | unreachable |
| 90d | 0.5% | -- | 41.34% | $1,357,669 | $11,860 |
| 90d | >=1.0% | proportion cap / par ceiling | -- | -- | unreachable |
| 30d and nearer | **all targets** | **par ceiling / proportion cap** | -- | -- | **unreachable at any price** |

**FACT.** Two distinct hard constraints, distinguished explicitly in the model
because they mean different things: `MarketExchangeRateBelowOne` (the par
ceiling, a property of the PT) and `MarketProportionTooHigh` at 96% (a property
of the curve). 504 of 576 displacement targets across the stress surface are
blocked by one of them.

**FACT.** Deepening liquidity 4x raises the absolute cost of a given displacement
but leaves the *share of the pool* required unchanged -- the curve is
scale-invariant in its reserves. Depth changes the price of the attack, not its
visibility.

### 8.2 Transmission into the oracle

**FACT.** A 900s TWAP transmits `heldSeconds / 900` of a market displacement.
For the achievable 0.5% target:

| Held | Fraction of window | Oracle displacement | Sustained cost (lower bound) | Cost per bp of oracle move |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12s (one block) | 0.013 | 0.0067% | $10,600 | $15,940 |
| 90s | 0.100 | 0.0499% | $10,600 | $2,125 |
| 225s | 0.250 | 0.1248% | $10,600 | $850 |
| 450s | 0.500 | 0.2497% | $10,600 | $425 |
| 900s | 1.000 | 0.5000% | $10,600 | $212 |
| 1800s | 2.000 | 0.5000% | $21,201 | $424 |

**FACT.** Through a linear-discount leg the transmission factor is exactly 0, at
any hold time and any cost.

**INFERENCE.** A single-block operation -- the only kind a flash loan can
finance -- yields under 1bp of oracle movement for a full round-trip cost. Getting
the full 0.5% requires holding 12.6% of the pool's PT for a complete 900s window,
exposed to arbitrage the entire time. That is a funded directional position, not
an atomic exploit.

## 9. Morpho collateral accounting

**FACT**, from `morpho-blue/src/Morpho.sol` and `libraries/MathLib.sol`,
reproduced in `models/morpho-market.js`:

```
maxBorrow = collateralAmount * oraclePrice / 1e36 * LLTV
```

- Elasticity of borrow capacity to oracle price is exactly **1.0** at every LLTV
  tested, verified to 1e-9. Nothing intervenes between `price()` and the health
  check.
- Morpho Blue uses **one** LLTV per market as both borrow threshold and
  liquidation threshold -- no separate liquidation threshold, no health-factor
  buffer between them.
- `LIF = min(1.15, 1 / (1 - 0.3 * (1 - LLTV)))`. At live PT-market LLTVs this
  yields **1.0438** (86%), **1.0262** (91.5%), **1.0168** (94.5%). The 1.15 cap
  never binds here. Small incentives make liquidators unusually sensitive to
  oracle error on these markets.
- **FACT.** Collateral is tracked as `position[id][onBehalf].collateral`, credited
  from the amount passed to `supplyCollateral`. No pricing path reads
  `balanceOf`, so donation and ERC-4626-inflation shapes do not apply to the
  Morpho leg. (Established by source reading, not by model assertion -- see the
  note in `test/phase5/invariant-comparison.js`.)

Capacity per 1,000,000 PT of collateral:

| LLTV | LIF | Base capacity | +1% oracle | +5% oracle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 86.0% | 1.0438 | $831,399 | +$8,314 | +$41,570 |
| 91.5% | 1.0262 | $884,570 | +$8,846 | +$44,229 |
| 94.5% | 1.0168 | $913,572 | +$9,136 | +$45,679 |

**INFERENCE.** The extra capacity is extra *debt*, not attacker profit. It
converts to profit only if the debt can exceed the collateral's independent
value, which is the threshold derived in section 12.

## 10. Recursive leverage model

Loop: own PT -> supply as collateral -> borrow loan asset to the LLTV limit ->
buy PT on the Pendle market -> supply -> repeat. Each purchase is executed
against the real curve, so it pays fees and moves the price it is paying.

Anchor market, LLTV 91.5%, own capital = 1% of the pool's PT:

| Depth | Effective leverage | Frictionless bound | Debt | AMM fees | Self-inflicted price move | Solvent (reference) | Solvent (realizable) | Shortfall | Looper PnL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1.894 | 1.897 | $28,998 | $72 | +0.012% | yes | yes | $0 | -$918 |
| 2 | 2.712 | 2.719 | $55,512 | $139 | +0.024% | yes | yes | $0 | -$1,682 |
| 3 | 3.459 | 3.472 | $79,753 | $199 | +0.034% | yes | yes | $0 | -$2,383 |
| 5 | 4.767 | 4.796 | $122,168 | $305 | +0.053% | yes | yes | $0 | -$3,616 |
| 10 | 7.169 | 7.254 | $200,102 | $499 | +0.087% | yes | yes | $0 | -$5,901 |
| 20 | 9.671 | 9.874 | $281,306 | $702 | +0.123% | yes | yes | $0 | -$8,309 |

**FACT.** Leverage saturates at 9.67x against the naive `1/(1-LLTV)` ceiling of
11.76x, and each additional loop adds strictly less exposure than the last. The
gap to the frictionless bound at the same depth is the loop's own price impact
plus accumulated fees. Reporting the naive figure would overstate achievable
leverage by 22%.

The `Frictionless bound` column is the closed form of the same recursion with no
fee and no slippage. It is worth noting how it is derived, because the obvious
formula is wrong: each iteration borrows `u` of the *remaining* headroom
`LLTV*C - D`, and both `C` and `D` rise by that amount, so headroom decays by
`1 - u(1 - LLTV)` per iteration -- not by `LLTV*u`. The textbook sum of
`(LLTV*u)^k` understates the bound enough that the simulated loop appears to beat
it, which is how the error was caught; a regression test now asserts the bound
holds at every depth and converges to `1/(1-LLTV)`.

**FACT.** Looping is *unprofitable at every depth* on its own: the looper pays
fees and slippage and receives no offsetting gain. It is a yield strategy whose
carry must exceed those costs, not an attack primitive.

**FACT.** Solvency against an independent mark holds at every depth, and at every
depth combined with displacement. Leverage scales collateral and debt by the same
factor, so it multiplies the *capacity* gain from a displacement without
improving the *solvency* margin. Amplification of exposure is real; amplification
of insolvency is not.

**FACT.** The real cost of depth is concentration: the unwind discount grows with
depth because the whole position must leave through the same curve it entered.

## 11. Liquidation model

**FACT.** A liquidator pays `seized * oraclePrice / LIF` and receives collateral
it can sell for `seized * realizableValue`. It participates while
`realizable > oraclePrice / LIF`, i.e. while over-marking is smaller than the
incentive.

The model reports **two** liquidation scenarios, because they answer different
questions:

- **While the oracle stays displaced** -- above the break-even over-mark,
  liquidators rationally decline and the debt sits unrecovered.
- **After the displacement ends** -- the case that determines whether the
  protocol takes a *real* loss, since holding an AMM displaced costs money every
  block and arbitrage is continuous.

**FACT.** In the second scenario the protocol shortfall is $0 in all 720 stress
rows. The collateral covers the debt at its independently realizable price
everywhere on the surface, so a displacement that ends leaves no lasting loss.

**FACT.** Unwind discount versus position size on the anchor market (1.0262
liquidation incentive = 255bp of margin):

| Position (share of pool PT) | Realizable per PT | Discount | Inside the incentive? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1% | 0.964195 | 26bp | yes |
| 5% | 0.963663 | 32bp | yes |
| 20% | 0.961732 | 52bp | yes |

The liquidation incentive at 91.5% LLTV is 255bp, so even a position equal to a
fifth of the entire Pendle pool leaves liquidators a 203bp margin.

**INFERENCE.** This is the genuine residual concern of the phase. It is a
liquidation-depth parameter question -- how large a single PT position a market
should permit relative to its Pendle pool -- and not an exploitable mismatch at
current sizes.

**Conservative assumptions used.** The realizable value assumes the entire
position exits through its own Pendle pool in one direction. Real liquidators may
use other venues, OTC flow, or hold to maturity for par. The modelled figure is
therefore a lower bound on recovery, biased against the protocol.

## 12. Economic stress surfaces

720 rows sweeping oracle distortion x time to maturity x liquidity x LLTV x loop
depth: `{0, 1%, 2%, 5%, 10%} x {365, 90, 30, 7}d x {0.25, 1, 4}x liquidity x
{86, 91.5, 94.5}% x depth {1, 3, 10, 20}`. Full data in
`research/data/phase5-results.json` and
`research/data/phase5-results-stress-surface.csv`.

Every row reports the manipulated mark *and* an independent reference mark, so no
row can conclude anything from the oracle's own number.

| Outcome | Rows |
| --- | --- |
| Attacker profitable net of manipulation cost | **0 / 720** |
| Insolvent against the reference mark | **0 / 720** |

That 30.85x figure exceeds `1/(1-LLTV)` = 18.2x, and the reason is worth stating
rather than quoting the number: at a 10% displacement `LLTV * (1 + d)` = 1.0395 >
1, so the loop is nominally unbounded -- each iteration borrows more than the
collateral it buys is honestly worth. It is listed only to show what the
unconstrained model does, and it is a **counterfactual row**: a 10% displacement
is unreachable at 7 days, where the par ceiling caps upward movement at 0.20%.
Rows like it are the reason every row carries the reachability flags below.

Independent accounting, reported per row and never derived from the oracle:
`attackerPnL`, `protocolShortfall`, `oracleOvervaluation`,

**Rows are flagged when they are counterfactual.** A displaced oracle price above
1.0 asset/PT exceeds the redemption ceiling: no AMM-sourced displacement can
produce it and a `min()`-aggregated feed cannot report it. Such rows are marked
`oraclePriceExceedsRedemptionCeiling` and must not be read as reachable
scenarios. The 10% and several 5% displacement columns fall entirely in this

## 14. Falsification results

Asserted in `test/phase5/falsification.js`. Each test asserts the *finding*, so
if the underlying economics change the test breaks and the conclusion must be
revisited.

| # | Hypothesis | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | A small price displacement materially increases Morpho borrowing power | **SUPPORTED in mechanism, QUALIFIED in effect.** Capacity is exactly proportional to oracle price with no damping. But the gain is debt, not value, and is bounded by the LLTV haircut (9.29% at the anchor market). |
| **B** | The displacement can be produced economically | **FALSIFIED.** Impossible at any price for T <= 30d (par ceiling). Achievable at 365d only by taking down 12.6% of the pool for $7.8k, of which a single-block hold transmits under 1bp into the oracle. |
| **C** | Recursive looping creates insolvency | **FALSIFIED.** Solvent against independent marks at all depths 1-20, with and without the achievable displacement. Leverage scales collateral and debt together. |
| **D** | The collateral cannot be liquidated at the oracle-marked value | **SUPPORTED in part, FALSIFIED as an attack.** A real unwind discount exists (26bp at 1% of pool, 52bp at 20%) but stays inside the 255bp liquidation incentive at every size tested. |
| **E** | Manipulation remains viable net of all costs | **FALSIFIED.** Attacker PnL is negative in all 12 reachable configurations and all 720 stress rows. Two independent structural reasons, and at least one applies everywhere tested: on all 23 live markets insolvency requires `P_PT < LLTV`, which none satisfies; and where that condition *is* met (long-dated, high LLTV) the curve's proportion cap holds achievable displacement to 2% against 5.8% required. |

**Failed hypotheses are the primary result here.** The mechanism the brief
described is real and correctly identified; what defeats it is a pair of
constraints on how far a PT price can be pushed at all. Near maturity the PT
redemption ceiling against Morpho's LLTV haircut leaves the required displacement
300x larger than the available one. At long maturity the ceiling stops being
sufficient and the curve's 96% proportion cap takes over, still leaving a ~3x
margin. Both hold everywhere tested; they are different mechanisms and it is
worth knowing which one a given market depends on.

## 15. Confirmed facts

1. Morpho Blue's borrow capacity is exactly linear in `IOracle.price()`; no cap,
   band, deviation limit or damping intervenes.
2. Morpho Blue uses a single LLTV as both borrow and liquidation threshold.
3. `LIF = min(1.15, 1/(1 - 0.3(1 - LLTV)))`; at live PT LLTVs, 1.0168-1.0438.
4. `IOracle.price()` is `1e36`-scaled and absorbs the token decimals
   differential.
5. Morpho prices internal collateral accounting, never token balances.
6. Pendle's `MarketMathCore._getExchangeRate` reverts below par, capping the PT
   price at 1.0 asset at any cost.
7. Pendle's curve reverts above a 96% PT proportion, which caps achievable
   displacement at 2.0% (365d), 1.0% (180d) and 0.5% (90d) on the anchor market
   independently of capital.
8. Pendle market pricing reads `_storage`, not token balances.
9. 66 live Morpho markets use PT collateral, across 6 chains.
10. Zero of the 57 verifiable oracle paths contain an untimed AMM spot read.
11. Observed TWAP windows are 900s (all but one) and 1800s.
12. Observed linear discounts are 0.20/yr and 0.30/yr.
13. `OjoPTFeed` aggregates with `min()`.
14. The local AMM port reproduces live oracle answers on 23/23 markets to
    <=3.6e-5.
15. The oracle-vs-realizable gap narrows monotonically toward maturity (~325x
    from 365d to 1d).
16. Available upward displacement narrows monotonically toward maturity, to 0.03%
    at 1 day.
17. At a 365-day horizon and the anchor's 10.25% implied rate, PT prices at
    0.9026 -- below the 91.5% and 94.5% LLTVs in live use. 120 of 720 stress rows
    are in that regime.
18. Recursive leverage saturates below `1/(1-LLTV)` and is unprofitable at every
    depth on its own.
19. Across 720 stress rows: 0 profitable, 0 insolvent, 0 shortfall.

## 16. Inferences

1. `min()` aggregation, not the TWAP, is the binding defence where both are
   present -- pushing the AMM leg up hands control to the immovable leg.
2. The linear-discount feeds are conservative by design, not merely
   manipulation-resistant, and cost borrowers real capacity to buy the protocol
   margin.
3. `MetaOracleDeviationTimelock` is a governance circuit breaker on feed
   authority, not a price filter.
4. Displacement-driven insolvency requires `P_PT < LLTV`, a condition on price
   and parameters alone -- capital, liquidity and loop depth do not enter.
5. That condition is satisfied by long-dated, high-implied-rate PT on high-LLTV
   markets without any credit event, so the par ceiling cannot be treated as the
   universal defence. In that regime the binding defence is instead the Pendle
   curve's 96% proportion cap, which is a property of pool depth rather than of
   arithmetic.
6. The near-maturity regime is the *safest*, inverting the intuition that
   convergence pressure creates risk.
7. Depth changes an attack's price but not its visibility, since the required
   share of the pool is scale-invariant.
8. A flash-loan variant cannot repay itself: the per-PT capacity gain from an
   atomic displacement is smaller than the per-PT round-trip cost of producing
   it.

## 17. Speculation and untested hypotheses

Explicitly *not* established by this work, listed so they are not mistaken for
findings:

1. **HYPOTHESIS.** A market whose PT trades below its LLTV rests on Pendle pool
   depth alone. The arithmetic is derived here and the 365-day case is simulated
   (protection holds, via the proportion cap, with a 2.0% vs 5.8% margin), but the
   margin is a function of pool composition rather than a hard bound, and no
   attempt was made to find a pool composition that narrows it. A thinner or more
   PT-heavy pool at a long maturity and a high LLTV is the configuration most
   worth checking, and it was not exhaustively searched.
2. **HYPOTHESIS.** Correlated stress across several markets sharing one Pendle
   pool or one underlying could compound liquidation depth pressure. Not modelled;
   each market is treated independently.
3. **HYPOTHESIS.** *Downward* displacement to trigger liquidations of others,
   rather than upward displacement to overborrow, is the more natural attack on
   this architecture -- the par ceiling constrains only the upward direction.
   Out of scope here, and it targets borrowers rather than protocol solvency.
4. **HYPOTHESIS.** The 9 unverified external feeds may have materially weaker
   properties than the Pendle-native paths. Their self-reported descriptions
   suggest exchange-rate and bounded-TWAP designs, but nothing was verified.

## 18. Unknowns

1. **9 markets terminate in an unverified external feed** (8 HyperEVM kHYPE
   markets via RedStone-composed feeds, 1 Katana market via a Curve-pool TWAP).
   Mechanism, observation window, deviation limits and fallback behaviour are
   UNKNOWN and these markets are excluded from all quantitative conclusions.
   Notably, the Katana feed's own `description()` states it is *"upper bounded by
   the redemption value"* -- an independent third-party statement of the same par
   ceiling derived here from Pendle source, though still unverified.
2. **Reserve fee overrides.** `readState` was called with router `address(0)`,
   returning the non-overridden fee. Whitelisted routers may trade more cheaply,
   so modelled costs are upper bounds on the fee component.
3. **Off-chain keeper behaviour.** Whether and how fast MetaOracle primary/backup
   switching is exercised is operator policy, not observable from chain state.
4. **Liquidator capacity beyond the Pendle pool.** Other venues, OTC flow and
   hold-to-maturity are all unmodelled, all of which would improve recovery.
5. **Fixed-point exactness.** The port is validated to 3.6e-5 against live values
   but is not bit-exact; `forge` and `cast` were unavailable in this environment,
   so no Pendle differential-fuzz against the Solidity was performed. See the
   provenance document.
6. **Governance risk.** Whether market creators can point an existing market at a
   weaker oracle, and under what timelock, was not investigated.

## 19. Defensive implications

Ordered by what the evidence actually supports.

1. **Keep the `min()` aggregation.** It is the strongest defence observed and it
   is cheap. On the 28 markets using a bare TWAP, adding an immovable
   linear-discount leg under a `min()` would reduce the transmission of any
   market displacement to zero at no cost to a well-behaved borrower.
2. **Treat `P_PT > LLTV` as an explicit, monitored invariant.** It is the exact
   boundary between the two protection regimes, it is trivially checkable from
   data already on chain, and it degrades gradually. Crossing it is not exotic:
   any sufficiently long-dated PT at a high implied rate on a high-LLTV market is
   already the wrong side of it, with no credit event required. Markets on the
   wrong side are not exploitable today, but they depend on Pendle pool depth
   rather than on arithmetic, so they are the ones whose pool composition needs
   watching.

   Concretely, the highest-LLTV markets should carry the *shortest* maturities:
   a 94.5% LLTV needs a 5.82% displacement to break, and a PT priced above 0.945
   cannot supply it at any price.
3. **Size markets against their Pendle pool, not just in dollars.** The one real
   gap found is liquidation depth: a position large relative to its pool has an
   unwind discount that eventually exceeds the (small, 1.7-4.4%) liquidation
   incentive. The ratio of market collateral to Pendle pool depth is the
   parameter that matters, and it is not currently visible in the market
   parameters.
4. **Prefer longer TWAP windows for long-dated PT.** Available displacement is
   largest at long maturity, where the par ceiling is furthest away. 900s is
   ample for the observed markets; it is the long-dated end that benefits most
   from more.
5. **Verify the 9 external feeds.** They are the only remaining gap in this
   analysis, and they carry live borrow.
6. **Do not rely on the LLTV haircut alone as the defence.** It happens to be the
   binding constraint here, but it is a static parameter defending against a
   dynamic quantity. The ceiling and the `min()` are the robust parts.

## 20. Conclusion

The hypothesis is **not supported**, and the reason is worth more than the
verdict.

The mechanism the brief identified is real: Morpho Blue transmits collateral
price into borrowing power with elasticity exactly 1 and no damping whatsoever,
so *if* a PT price could be displaced, borrowing power would follow it precisely.
That is the same mechanism the lab's earlier Moola phases exploited successfully.

What differs is the price source. In the Moola phases the oracle performed one
untimed spot read of an AMM, and the count of such reads across all 57 verifiable
Morpho PT oracle paths is zero. But time-weighting turns out not to be the
decisive defence either. What actually defeats the attack is that a PT price
cannot be pushed far enough, for either of two reasons depending on the market.

Near maturity the reason is arithmetic: a PT cannot be priced above par, so the
available displacement is `1/P - 1` while the displacement required to make a
maximally-borrowed position insolvent is `1/LLTV - 1`. The attack needs
`P_PT < LLTV`. Every live market with a verifiable feed sits on the safe side of
that, and the margin *widens* as maturity approaches -- exactly opposite to the
hypothesis.

Long-dated PT is where this analysis had to be corrected mid-flight, and the
correction is the most useful thing in it. `P_PT < LLTV` is not a remote
condition requiring a credit event: at a 365-day horizon and the anchor market's
own implied rate, PT prices at 0.9026, below both the 91.5% and 94.5% LLTVs in
live use. In that regime the par ceiling provides no margin at all and protection
falls to a second, unrelated mechanism -- the Pendle curve's 96% PT proportion
cap, which holds achievable displacement to 2.0% against the 5.8% required. That
still holds, with roughly a 3x margin, everywhere tested. But it is a property of
pool depth and composition rather than of arithmetic, and it is the part of this
result that could change without anything being deployed or exploited.

This is therefore not a new vulnerability class. It is the stale/lagging oracle
class with the sign reversed: the conservative feeds lag downward, which protects
the protocol at the borrower's expense.

One genuine finding survives falsification, and it is a sizing question rather
than a vulnerability: PT collateral has exactly one native liquidation venue, so
a position's unwind discount is a function of its size relative to its own Pendle
pool. At current sizes that discount stays inside the liquidation incentive with
room to spare (52bp against 255bp at a fifth of the pool).

Both surviving concerns point at the same unwatched quantity. Liquidation depth
and the long-dated displacement cap are both functions of market collateral
versus Pendle pool depth -- a ratio that is not part of a Morpho market's declared
risk parameters and is not visible in the market map without joining two data
sources. That is the one number this analysis suggests should be monitored, and
the suggested pairing is straightforward: the highest LLTVs belong on the
shortest maturities, where the par ceiling still does the work by itself.
